If you've booked your first session, or you're thinking about it, feeling nervous is completely normal. Many people spend months, sometimes years, wondering whether their problems are "big enough" for therapy. The fact that you're here already says something about your courage.

Here's what a first session usually looks like, so there are fewer surprises.

## Before the session

Find a private spot where you won't be overheard: a closed room, a parked car, even a quiet terrace. Use headphones if you can. Check your internet connection a few minutes early, and keep some water nearby.

You don't need to prepare anything. If it helps, jot down a few words about what's been on your mind, but there's no script to follow.

## What happens in the first session

The first session is mostly about getting to know each other. Your therapist will probably:

- explain how sessions work, and what confidentiality means, including its limits
- ask what brought you here, in whatever words feel right to you
- ask a little about your life: family, work or studies, sleep, and what support you have
- talk about what you'd like to get out of therapy

You decide how much to share. It's fine to say "I'm not ready to talk about that yet." A good therapist will respect that.

## It's okay if it feels awkward

Talking to a stranger about personal things can feel strange at first, especially if you weren't raised to talk about feelings. That awkwardness usually fades as trust builds.

It's also okay to switch between Malayalam and English mid-sentence. Many people find some feelings are easier to say in one language than the other.

## After the session

You might feel lighter, or tired, or a bit unsettled. All of these are normal. Give yourself a quiet moment afterwards if you can.

If the therapist doesn't feel like the right fit, that's okay too. The relationship with your therapist matters, and it's fine to ask to see someone else.

Taking the first step is often the hardest part. Whatever made you consider it, you deserve support.
